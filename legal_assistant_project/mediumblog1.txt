Egyptian Labor Law - Core Contract Provisions

Article 1: Employment Contract
An employment contract is an agreement under which a worker works under the direction or supervision of an employer in exchange for compensation. The contract must be written and may be for a fixed or indefinite term.

Article 2: Non-compete Period
Article 11 of Egyptian labor law allows a non-compete clause if it has a defined term of no more than two years and a reasonable geographic scope. Any clause exceeding two years is invalid.

Article 3: Contract Termination
An employer may terminate an employment contract with at least three months' advance notice for experienced workers. Termination without notice requires compensation equal to the wages for the notice period.

Article 4: Compensation Upon Termination
A worker is entitled to compensation when the employer terminates the contract, amounting to at least one month's wages for each year of service. Excessive penalties exceeding the actual damage are legally invalid.

Article 5: Confidentiality
A confidentiality clause may protect business secrets, but it must be limited to information that is genuinely necessary and to a reasonable period of no more than five years after the contract ends. A lifetime confidentiality clause is excessive and may be challenged in court.

Article 6: Dispute Resolution
Labor disputes should preferably be settled before Egyptian courts under Egyptian labor law. An agreement granting jurisdiction to foreign courts or arbitration outside Egypt may restrict the worker and create high legal costs.

Article 7: Wages
Wages must be paid regularly and on the agreed dates. Workers have the right to review wage and deduction details. Any unjustified deduction violates labor law.

Article 8: Leave
A worker is entitled to at least 21 paid working days of annual leave after one year of service, increasing to 30 days after ten years of service.

Article 9: Workplace Injury Rights
Employers must provide health insurance and cover workplace injuries. An employment contract may not be terminated while a worker is on sick leave resulting from a workplace injury.

Article 10: Penalties and Sanctions
Contractual penalties must be proportionate to the actual damage. Astronomical penalties exceeding the contract value or actual damage are abusive and may be reduced by a court.

International Provisions in Employment Contracts:
When the parties agree to apply foreign law, they should consider that this may deprive the worker of protections guaranteed by Egyptian law. Egyptian courts generally apply public policy and protect workers from unfair terms even in international contracts.

Arbitration Clauses:
Arbitration clauses must be clear and specific. Arbitration in London or New York can create prohibitive costs that prevent a worker from claiming their rights. Arbitration in Egypt under the rules of the Cairo Regional Centre for International Commercial Arbitration is generally preferable.

Important Legal Definitions:
- Unfair clause: A term that unjustly favors one party at the expense of the other.
- Public policy: Fundamental principles that cannot be overridden by agreement.
- Unfair termination: Termination without a lawful reason or the required notice.
- Agreed compensation: An amount agreed in advance as compensation for breach of contract.
